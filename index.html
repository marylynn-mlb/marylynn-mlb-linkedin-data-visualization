LinkedIn data export

My LinkedIn data, visualized

By Mary-Lynn Bragg · Built with Claude Code · October 2026

LinkedIn holds far more about my professional life than its interface shows. I downloaded my full data export and asked twelve questions of it: who is in my network, what LinkedIn thinks it knows about me, who is waiting on a reply, and how my connections grew through each job.

Privacy. Everything about other people has been removed or anonymized. No names, no message text, no contact details. Numbers, categories and organization names remain.

        New connections (bars) and posts (gold dots) by month

        More detail

        Average new connections by posts in the month

        Correlation checks

        Takeaway. There is a real but moderate link: posts explain about 12% of the month-to-month swings. It does not look like posts causing growth, because a post in one month does not predict more connections the next. More likely something else raises both at once, such as conference season (ASU+GSV in April, EdTech Week in October) or a big announcement. With only 47 posts in 14 years, this is a pattern worth noting, not proof.

        Sources: Shares.csv, Connections.csv. Only current connections are counted, so older months are slightly understated. Significance comes from a 5,000-shuffle permutation test.

        How this was made

        I requested my complete data export from LinkedIn and asked Claude Code twelve questions about it. Claude wrote the analysis, and I reviewed the results and directed the design. Standard charts use Chart.js and network graphs use D3.js, in Inter. Industries, themes and message types were assigned with keyword rules and hand review, so treat the percentages as approximate. Anything about other people was removed or anonymized before publishing.
