How do I get my LinkedIn export?
  1. On LinkedIn, open Settings & Privacy → Data privacy → Get a copy of your data. Menu names change from time to time.
  2. Choose Want something in particular? and tick Connections, Messages, Invitations, Posts (Shares), Positions and Company Follows, or choose the full archive.
  3. Click Request archive. LinkedIn emails you a link, usually within minutes for a small export and up to 24 hours for the full one.
  4. Download the .zip and drop it above. No need to unzip it.

Go deeper with your own AI

Six more analyses need judgment: reading messages, grouping companies, interpreting what LinkedIn inferred. Four more turn your results into a plan, with names, for your eyes only. Hand them to an AI assistant, with this kit.

The starter kit

One download with everything an AI assistant needs: standing instructions that keep other people's names out of the results and match your chosen colors and fonts, plus ten ready-made prompts, including a reconnection list and hello drafts.

  1. Unzip the kit into the folder that holds your unzipped LinkedIn export, so the prompts sit next to files like Connections.csv.
  2. Point your AI at that folder. Use an AI assistant that can read files on your computer, such as Claude Code, and start it from inside that folder (or choose that folder when it asks where to work). That is how it reads your CSV files without you uploading them by hand.
  3. Say “Read START-HERE.md and run analysis 1.”
A word on privacy. This page never sends your data anywhere, but an AI assistant is different. Once you point it at your export, it reads those files and sends what it reads to the company that runs the AI, under that company’s data policy. That is true of every prompt below, so check your assistant’s privacy settings first (for example, turn off training on your data and use a private or temporary session). private marks the prompts that also put real names of people in your network into the results, which is what makes them useful for reaching out. Treat those most carefully: don’t post, email or share the files, and skip them if you aren’t comfortable. The unmarked prompts tell the AI to keep names out of its results, but it still sees the raw files.

Or copy a single prompt

Chat-only assistants cannot read large CSV files. If you use one, paste only small anonymized samples, never other people's messages. Check any AI tool's data policy first: unlike this page, an AI service processes whatever you give it.

How this works

This page is a single static web page that you open in your browser, and it runs only on your computer. It isn’t hosted anywhere and has no server: the file you opened is the whole app.

How to use it

  • Drop in your LinkedIn export (the .zip, the unzipped folder, or the CSV files).
  • Your browser’s own file reader opens the files and everything is analyzed in memory on your device. Nothing is uploaded or stored.
  • Restyle it with Colors & fonts, save your summary card as an image, or save the whole report as an HTML file you can keep or share.
  • Save your report before you leave. This page stores nothing, so your results exist only while it is open. If you close the tab, refresh it, or the app is updated behind the scenes, they are gone and you would need to drop your export in again. Save report (.html) downloads a self-contained file named like network-atlas-2026-10-04.html. It opens in any browser, works offline because it carries its own copy of the chart libraries, and keeps looking the way it did when you saved it, even if the app changes later. It is a snapshot, so it doesn’t update. It holds counts, categories and organization names, but no names of people and no message text. The AI starter kit stays in the app and is not part of the file.
  • The sample data is made up, so you can look around first.

How it is built

  • Charts use Chart.js and network graphs use D3.js. The zip is opened with JSZip.
  • The analysis is plain JavaScript. Industries are assigned by simple keyword rules, so treat them as approximate.
  • Relationship tiers come from message dates and senders only. Message text is never read.
  • The only things loaded from the internet are the chart libraries and fonts, never your data.
